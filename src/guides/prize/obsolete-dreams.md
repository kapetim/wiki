# 🧮 Obsolete dreams — the abacus read

**What this is:** what happens when a **fair, hard competition** gets outsmarted by a **calculator** — the dream doesn't fail, it becomes **irrelevant**. The competitor trained a lifetime; the machine
one-shots it. Pair read with futures.md (automation fork) and mastery-axis.md (what the world asks you to master). **Metaphor only.**

---

## ⏳ Timing — skills have a lifespan

A skill has a **window**. Get good at a hard, emerging thing **before everyone else** and it fuels the pursuit;
arrive late and the same skill becomes old research — real, but no longer motivating. "Being good at maths" was
valuable right up to the moment AI made everyone good at it. Nobody wants to learn to be a calculator anymore —
they want to build a spaceship. **Find the current hard thing and move on it while it is still hard.**

---

## 🧮 The abacus read — outsmarted by the calculator

An **abacus competition** is a beautiful thing: fair rules, real skill, decades of craft. Then the **calculator** arrives, and it's over — not because the competitors got worse, but because the
contest stopped meaning anything.

- The **pleasure was the fair fight** — human vs human, cleanest approach wins.
- The **calculator removes the fight** — no human opponent can beat it.
- The dream didn't fail. It was **obsoleted**.

Same move, different era: the **jousting knight, the clockmaker, the memorization champion.** The skill was real; the arena was replaced.

---

## ⚔️ Worked example — competitive programming

**The context:** studied to be a **problem solver** — the fair, hard thing that felt like the peak of intellectual competition.

**The timeline:**

- ~1 year of work → **1750 rating** — roughly top ~80% of competitors with modest effort.
- Since ~**2024**, **cheap models solve most Div2 problems** on Codeforces — instantly, and with clean code.
- The "who solves it first, with the cleanest approach" contest is now **human vs model**, and the model wins every time.

**Why it stung:** it wasn't a loss — it was the arena being **dismantled** while you were still training in it.

> Like an abacus champion told the calculator exists. Nothing you practiced stopped working. The game just stopped being worth keeping score in.

---

## 🏋️ What survives — the resistance model

The arena died, but **you** were forged in it. The heavy-weight model:

> Train with heavy weights in the gym, and **supermarket chores feel light**. Very hard to face a purchase as heavy as the plates you used to lift.

Competitive programming built a **durable generalist edge**:

- Trained how to **learn hard things fast** — an easier-to-find trait.
- Proof of **stick-with-it** — the discipline transfers to anything.
- A calibrated **fairness sense** — you know what a real contest looks like vs a rigged one.
- Being a **smart generalist** — most other things feel easier than that competition, so you're **more resistant than average**.

The dream became obsolete. The **training** didn't.
