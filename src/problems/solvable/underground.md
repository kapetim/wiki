# 🕳️ Underground — the untraceable wheel

**What this is:** a **perpetual-damage** wheel — the underground as a place to disappear. The police watch the surface; the paths below it are unmonitored, so a person can vanish by walking.

## 📋 Problem statement

<!-- begin table -->
| Axis | Answer | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem** | A hidden path network under a city lets people move and disappear untraced | the subway and tunnels as an unmonitored graph |

| **Time to implement** | Weeks of patient digging per connection | cheap tools, slow buildout |

## ⚙️ The wheel model

- **The disguise entry** — many look-alike entrants (drag queens, clowns), then the costume is dropped and the crowd is all normal people; no visual proof of who did it.
- **The hidden door** — leave through a tunnel, plaster the opening, rebuild the wall; the police lose days finding it.
- **The swap** — enter the metro, take a passenger's clothes, walk out looking like them; the camera sees a person who entered and left.
- **The underground graph** — basements connect through paths; a store becomes an exit upstairs. While a duplicate path exists, one can be blocked and the runner escapes.
- **The rabbit-hole race** — snakes (police) versus rabbits (criminals); the rabbits only need to hide faster than the police find them.

## 🏛️ The ownership turn

- Once enough crimes run through it, the government eventually finds and takes over the system — which may be how the first tunnels and railways started.
- Police are slow and pick robust tools; criminals accept cheap tricks and dirt for one more night of sleep.

## ❓ Open questions

- How much of a city is genuinely unmonitored, and for how long?
- Does surveillance ever catch a path network, or only the people who surface?
- When the government takes over, does the wheel close or only change owner?
