# 🪙 Karma — the accounting system

**What this is:** karma as **accounting** — every person carries a **life log** with two columns: what they **deserve
and have not received**, and what they **should have paid and have not**. When the ledger cannot pay, **vengeance
masquerades as a payment** ([fair-game.md](fair-game.md) runs the trial).

## 🧾 The ledger — two sides

Every person carries a running account:

<!-- begin table -->
| Column | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Receivable** | what they **deserve but have not received** |
| **Payable** | what they **should have paid but have not** |
<!-- end table -->

- **Easy cases** — a **restaurant** has well-defined prices and payment methods, and the **police** run after people
  who do not pay the bill.
- **Hard truth** — most crimes carry **no consequence**: no receipt, no counterparty, no enforcer.

## 💳 Debt and credit

- **Bad karma = a debt** — a potential crime with a cost; the price is set by fairness and law.
- **Good karma = credit** — a good act earns standing; the ledger tilts positive.
- **Police = the enforcer** — they hold the stick but rarely the proof; without constant watch, most harm is
  unprovable ([evidence.md](evidence.md), [monitoring.md § police-monitoring paradox](../monitoring.md)).

## 🎯 The 1:1 dream

**1:1 karma** is the dream: **spend 1, pay 1** — an *equal* response to the act, the books in balance. It is the goal
everyone wants and the hardest to reach. In practice it is **somewhat easy within a group of reversible actions** and
**impossible with another** group.

## ♻️ Reversible vs impossible

- **Reversible** — the harm can be given back: return the money, repair the thing, heal the wound. Here 1:1 is
  reachable; the ledger can actually balance.
- **Irreversible** — nothing can be given back, so 1:1 is **impossible**. The books never balance.

Examples of the impossible group:

<!-- begin table -->
| Case | Why 1:1 fails |
| --- | --- |
| **Serial killers** | one killer, many victims — no single payment clears many erased runs |
| **Twin Towers** | one act ends thousands of lives — what equal response balances that? |
| **Accidental catastrophes** | no culprit to pay — nobody chose the harm |
<!-- end table -->

## 🩺 The impossible category

Health and death are the cleanest examples of "cannot be redone":

- **Killed** — the assassinated would need to be **revived**. Not possible.
- **HIV** — the sender already has it; giving it "back" is just **spreading their love**.
- **Abortion** — a weird category; there is nothing to price cleanly.

## 🧍 The victim's dilemma

Once you are a **victim**, believing in karma is **fooling yourself** — the ledger has no enforcer and no revival.

- At best you pursue **vengeance**; it does not help you either.
- **Not attacking back** is a **coin of fairness** — it looks dumb, but it works as **credit** for the future, a
  **good-faith** gesture.
- The account is **not zero-sum**: the coin costs you nothing but keeps the good faith alive.

## 🐄 The farmer's debt

If everyone pursued vengeance, a **farm owner** carries enormous debt — every animal killed, every plant cut. The debt
is real only inside a context; **debts are contextual, not absolute**.

## 🗳️ A crafted fairness system

Not a cosmic ledger — a **crafted fairness system**, partly **vote-based**, where people **trade by interacting**.
Satisfaction is **subjective**: you can be **punched and still happy with the prize trophy**; **kidnapped and then
handed a job offer**, and you forget it.

## 💰 Match the payment to the victim

The system's job: the **culprit pays what is needed**, and the **victim receives what is deserved**. Most of the time
it is not perfect — but it is **something**.

- **Serial killers** carry a huge debit — but **torture**-based payment is useless to a family that wants **money**,
  and vice versa.
- A **rich** family may want torture; a **poor** family wants **money**.
- So: identify what is **fair for the victim** and give back **what was taken** — in the currency they actually value.

## ⚖️ Minimum-viable compensation

Alternative to chasing a fair ledger: **scan for the minimum viable routine per individual** that makes the complaint
go away. If a family member died and the family **agreed 100k was enough to keep their mouth shut** — so be it. The
government pays the amount; there's **no complaint about unfairness**.

<!-- begin table -->
| Case | Deal | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family member died | Family accepts 100k silence | Government pays; grievance closed |
<!-- end table -->

Fairness becomes a **negotiated number** instead of a cosmic ledger. Refreshing to think about — very complex to handle.

## ⚠️ The compensation trap

The scan prices death — and that prices **killing**: a poor guy kills his family member and **every kill counts as a
100k bonus to his bank account**. The compensation model converts murder into a **payout** and creates the incentive it
was meant to remove.

## ⚖️ The fairness ceiling

Even with vengeance as the default, the books cannot balance:

- **Mass casualty** — the Twin Towers. One act, thousands of runs ended. What equal response balances that?
- **Accidents during tests** — nobody chose harm; deaths still happened. What does karma owe? To whom?
- **One killer, many victims** — one person ends multiple runs. **Unless people revive, karma is mathematically
  impossible** → infinite unfairness.

## 🌫️ The grey area

Between the easy cases and the closed ones sits the **grey area**: the culprit is **unknown and possibly alive**, but
there is **no proof** — at best a **camera** showing a masked person.

- The ledger says **owed**; the evidence says **maybe**
  ([online.md](../../problems/unknown/online.md), [surveillance.md](../../problems/unknown/surveillance.md)).

## 🕯️ Closed cases — judged impossible

Events that **already happened and cannot be judged**, because the people who could owe or collect are **already dead**:

<!-- begin table -->
| Event | Date | Scale | Score | Why unjudgeable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bin Laden / 9-11** | 2001-09-11 (killed 2011-05-02) | 2,977 victims (2,996 incl. 19 hijackers) | 1 : 2,977 | both sides dead — no equal response exists |
| **TAM 3054 / Congonhas** | 2007-07-17 | 199 dead (187 aboard + 12 ground) | — | rainy-runway overrun — no culprit left to charge |
<!-- end table -->

These are a **record of what already happened**, not a verdict ([hard-limit.md](../fundamentals/hard-limit.md)).
