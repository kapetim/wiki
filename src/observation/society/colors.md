# 🎨 Colors — the persuasion groups

**What this is:** a framework of groups, each with a color, describing how people respond to weird events and where the full picture goes missing. **Metaphor only.** Pairs with
[prejudice.md](../prejudice.md) — a label is a hypothesis.

## 💗 Pink — the cheap band-aid

Neither pink nor orange seems that impactful; both operate like a psychological band-aid. When facing some weird event, pink-group people offer a cheap explanation that generally lacks details. Like
the monitoring shit: if you watch
Truman, people claim lots of cameras recording him — but what about travelling by airplane between countries, or being in the desert? They even create Starlink to keep the lie that life is a video
game and everyone is monitored.

If they were truly capable of manipulating people, there would be no police with guns nor criminals — just influence everyone to not commit crimes. It works well with
stupid people who don't want to see the full picture. If you realize there's no camera — your eyesight is the camera — then you realize there's no privacy.

## 🟠 Orange — the forged alternate reality

Orange may be for those who can't believe the pink idea of simply doing what they say — they try to craft an alternate reality. Like a crime scene: pink claims the crime never happened; orange says
someone else did it and tries to forge
proofs to hide their shit. None of these groups provides the full picture — it helps hold the lie for longer while someone enjoys a victory for a while: kill someone, put it into someone else's
account, and continue life.

## ⚪⚫ White and black — the truth keepers

White and black hold onto the truth and fate — taking notes of good and bad realistic facts, like those who watch the live stream and store the good and bad events. Not necessarily useful — they just
know the truth but don't do shit.

## 🟣🟢 Purple and green — karma as action

Purple and green work as some kind of karma — those who have the courage to punch and kill. Still highly debatable: what if someone lies about the target? Person A was the culprit but they attack
person B — more unfairness.

## ⚪ Gray — the evaluator

Gray may be a middle point to hold some karma — the combination of facts, useful for producing an evaluation. It should be like a financial balance: if you get punched, you should be granted credit to
punch
back the one who hit you. Pretty when dealing with recoverable things — but what if they put HIV into you, or remove all your limbs? What about serial killers — revive the killer N times?

Life is like a game where anyone takes the action they find most suitable. When two don't stop, it triggers war: A attacks B, B counters more aggressively, up to death. The last attacker won the
battle but becomes a karma target — some value put on your death.

Killing a criminal may look good, but then you're the new killer. If there were only one group it would make sense, but it's like hell and heaven — person X from heaven kills
person Y, person Z from hell liked Y and kills X, a new A from heaven kills Z. The wheel keeps spinning, only differing in time between deaths, triggered by events.

It's joining enough energy against someone and finding a person who can trigger it. When efficient, they shoot the target with no sentiment; when not, they let the
person slowly die from some unknown reason — a bad habit followed by an eventual accident, like racing while being reckless about maintenance.

## 🐭 Disney and the first-mover

Disney represents the pink group well — beautiful movies that have a meaning. If you look carefully, there's a reason White Snow has this name — fantasy on top of a woman who loves cocaine and hangs
out
with small-dick friends. Felix the Cat may be similar — the male version of a person who can't cope with rigid routines, constantly avoiding problems caused by an envious individual.

Gray is the realistic shit — a documentary about Walt Disney showing the good and bad parts: was poor, worked hard, found a combination of sketches people liked, sold it, made money, kept delivering.
McDonald's did the same — found a magic recipe and repeated it with efficiency.

Interestingly, the first to hit jackpot tends to keep winning for longer — an inherent teacher. Others are complainers copying without understanding the real motivation. Uber also — more used than
other options.

The first to create has a benefit of the imagination: if the idea is new, you probably always win against competitors with the same resources — they lack the creativity
that helped you make the first move. Still, McDonald's success came from something else than the creator — like having your recipe stolen once found.

Regardless of the source, both groups use other people's history as entertainment for others, potentially inspiring them. Despite their differences, both can be watched and comprehended differently
depending on the viewer and their point of view.
