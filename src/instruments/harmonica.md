# 🎵 Harmonica mapping

Single-line melody translation for diatonic harmonica practice.

## 👀 What to pay attention to when playing

Track these while practicing. A good harp is easy to obtain; **bends and rhythm** are the ones to master.

- **Blow / draw** — steady direction changes.
- **Breath** — diaphragm support; don't gulp air.
- **Bends** — draw bends (and overblows) in tune — the hard one.
- **Tongue blocking / pucker** — clean single notes.
- **Hand wah** — cupping the harp for the wah effect, plus vibrato.
- **Dynamics** — soft to loud.
- **Rhythm** — phrasing — the hard one.

## 🗺️ Core layout

- Treat harmonica as melody-first (similar to flute).
- Keep letter note targets plus numbered breath holes.
- Mark blow (`B`) vs draw (`D`) where relevant.

## 🎵 Notation rule

- Numbered holes with direction (`4B`, `4D`, etc.).
- Letter melody kept beside hole notation.

## 🎵 Example song: Song of Storms (multi-part practice)

- Part A letters: `D F D F D F E C`
- Part B letters: `D F A G F E D C`
- Part C letters: `A C A C A C G E`

Example hole-style sketch (for practice memory, adjust to your key/harp):

```text
Part A: 4D 5D 4D 5D 4D 5D 5B 4B
Part B: 4D 5D 6D 6B 5D 5B 4D 4B
Part C: 6D 7D 6D 7D 6D 7D 6B 5B
```

Use this as a relative pattern template; exact holes vary by harmonica key.

## 🎵 Full-song arrangement template

```text
Harp key:

Intro:

Part B:

```

Checklist:

1. For each section, store hole-direction notation and letter notes together.
2. Mark bends/overblows explicitly only where needed.
3. Keep section labels aligned with flute and piano for cross-check.
