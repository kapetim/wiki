# ⏳ Hard limit — life ends by force or organ failure

**What this is:** the one fact under all fear work — **life has a hard limit**. The run ends either because **someone forces it** (another person ends it), or because you
**naturally die of organ failure**. Everything else is arranging the time in between. Pairs with [harm.md](./harm.md) (the ledger) and [judgement.md](../perception/judgement.md) (A–E tiers).
**Metaphor only.**

---

## 💥 The two exits

<!-- begin table -->
| Exit | Who | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forced** | Someone else ends the run | Assassination, murder, violence — **not chosen by you** |
| **Natural** | Your own body | Organ failure, time, biology — the default close |
<!-- end table -->

**No revival** in this model — death is not undone, only prevented *before*. See [restart.md](../framework/restart.md) for the after-life
question.

---

## 🎭 Death by another human — usually a mistake

Most people don't keep killing everyone. When a run ends by another person, it's **generally a mistake**:

<!-- begin table -->
| Cause | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| **Lack of attention** | Carelessness, negligence — no intent to end the run |
| **Anger** | Heat of the moment — intent, but not a plan |
| **Planned** | Rare — someone's game was made around **vengeance**, and killing means **victory** |
| (cont.) | It happens, but **occasionally** |
<!-- end table -->

The mistake is the norm; the plan is the exception. Fear work should weigh both, but not treat every stranger as the third kind.

---

## 🍴 Accidents are very hard to prevent

A **fork** was made to eat food — and can be used to blind someone. Tools have intended uses and **all the other uses**. This is the accident problem in one object:

- You cannot prevent every misuse of every object.
- Some ends are pure bad luck — physics, timing, another's error.
- Margins reduce the tail; they do not delete it.

So: **prevent what's preventable, accept the irreducible tail, and don't rehearse the unpreventable.**

---

## 🧬 The body can change shape

Accident, choice, or force can change the **body plan** — amputation, transition, mutilation. The person inside persists; the **shape** doesn't undo. Same hard-limit family: some changes have no
"back" ([judgement § duration](../perception/judgement.md#-duration--cure-maintenance-or-forever)).

---

## ♾️ The infinite tier — prevention only

The hardest exits (death, irreversible harm) sit at **−∞** on the ledger — **no known cure**. You cannot "earn back" a run that ended:

- **Prevention is the only lever** — and it's a *before* lever.
- After: grief, repair of what remains, next margin — not grinding +1 in rumination.

Severity ranking and cure tiers: [judgement.md](../perception/judgement.md).

## 🦿 Unrecoverable problems — accept, then decorate

An accident may delete a leg — until some bone regrows, impossible to undo. At best you **decorate that person's life with tools** to help them keep walking and reach their goals. Accept that life is
beautiful — until you hit an unrecoverable problem; the quicker you accept it, the better.

Life is pretty much different, but not totally:

- Bolt loses his leg → impossible to run as before; **forbidden from the same category**, regardless of the replacement tool.
- Some tricks make it look very similar — but it rarely is. If it were possible, people would suffer less.
- Same applies to HIV and spine problems.

Better to comprehend **which things you can't do and why**. With that in mind, follow your own rules and pick a better routine to match your needs.

---

### 🎬 Destiny — movie or game

Thinking about destiny is complex — sounds like a waste of time.

- Does a person lose a leg to be *forced* to keep sitting, or keep sitting as a consequence of losing the leg? What about accidents?
- If life is a **movie**, a director planned it — you can't do shit.
- If it's a **game**, it's somewhat random. Anyone can do anything, but most people don't do shit. From time to time they create a war, forget about it, have sex, and raise children.
