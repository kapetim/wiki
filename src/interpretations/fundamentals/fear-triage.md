# 🎯 Fear triage — top fears first

**What this is:** the practical rule for fear and rumination —
**understand the things you fear the most and avoid those happening; the rest are less relevant since you can probably handle them with ease.** Plus the **"is further thinking useful?"** template for
any worry, the boot essence for acute spin-up, the **"mood ≠ forecast"** stance, and the opposite-pole note. Pairs with [harm.md](./harm.md) (the loop) and [monitoring.md](../perception/monitoring.md)
(paranoia). **Metaphor only.**

## 🎯 The triage rule

Not all fears deserve the same attention:

<!-- begin table -->
| Fear | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| **Top fears** | Understand them deeply — then **actively avoid** those outcomes |
| **The rest** | Notice, don't furnish — you can handle them with ease |
<!-- end table -->

Spend fear-work where the consequence is real and avoidable. The rest is noise that steals the day.

## 🔎 "Is further thinking useful?" — template

For any concern, run this once. The goal is **not to prove or disprove**, but to decide **whether more thinking is useful**.

1. **Question** — state the concern in one sentence.
2. **Motivation** — what decision would change if the answer differed?
3. **Current evidence** — observations only, no interpretations.
4. **Assumptions** — what must be true for the concern to hold?
5. **Alternative explanations** — simpler ones: bias, coincidence, memory, pattern-matching.
6. **Worst case** — assume it's true; what's the practical consequence?
7. **Best case** — assume it's false; what changes?
8. **Actionability** — can you run an experiment? If no, accept uncertainty and continue.
9. **Cost of investigation** — five minutes, a day, or *forever*? Beware no stopping condition.
10. **Infinite-staircase check** — can every answer generate another question? If no termination, don't climb.
11. **Evidence required to revisit** — what concrete observation would reopen this? Without it, don't.
12. **Practical strategy** — regardless of the hypothesis, what behavior is still optimal? Do that.
13. **Opportunity cost** — what does the thinking cost you elsewhere?

**Default policy:**

```text
accept uncertainty → record concern → continue normal routine
→ revisit only if new evidence appears
```

## ⚡ Boot essence — acute spin-up

When fear or anxiety blocks function, the fastest path is a short sequence — not solving the whole graph:

1. **Name the state** — one label, no story yet (anxiety · loop · shame · anger/grief).
2. **Body first** (2–5 min) — slow breath, water, stand/walk, cold water. Chemistry shifts before cognition.
3. **Delay irreversible** — no messages, money moves, bridges, or big decisions until you're down.
4. **One outward step** — one smallest physical action ([play § one outward step](../framework/play.md#-one-outward-step-after-spin-down)).

Boot is done when **one next action is in motion**.

## 🌤️ "Mood ≠ forecast"

The protective stance from the old voluntary-death read:

- Suicidal thoughts and dark moods surface odd ideas — treat them as **noise unless immediate risk**; they are not reliable predictions of tomorrow.
- **Predicting tomorrow is hard**; horror fantasies are not forecasts.
- Default: there is usually **another move** — wait, escape, help, distraction, change the environment.
- If acting on self-harm right now: crisis line or emergency services — this note is for later stabilization.

## ↔️ Understand opposites (and take notes)

The method that replaced the old trait rankings: every concept has a **pole**, and knowing where things sit is more useful than memorizing categories.

- Sprint ↔ distance, sentimental ↔ detached, impulsive ↔ disciplined, wooden ↔ compelling.
- **Know where you are** on the axes that matter for you — and take notes as you learn them.
- Personal fit drives the routine ([pleasure § personal fit](../framework/pleasure.md#-personal-fit--not-everyone-suits-everything)).
