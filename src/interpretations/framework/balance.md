## ⚡ Powers object vs person

Power can be **attached** two ways — and the attachment decides how easy it is to steal.

<!-- begin table -->
| Attach | Example | Losing it | Theft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Object** | Millennium Items (Yu-Gi-Oh!) | Item can be stolen; prophecy tends to return it to the owner | Possible — but worthless without the frame |
| **Person** | Naruto kekkei genkai (bloodline) | **Impossible to lose** | No — it is the body |
<!-- end table -->

**Power allocated by nature.** In a monitored sim world, everyone is *tested since birth* and matched with a power that fits their characteristics. You are shaped by those who raise you — same
choices, similar
lines of thought — so the object is **designed for the person**: Yugi's childhood is why the Puzzle chose him. The creator makes the item, but someone must be responsible for using it well.

**Objects are decorations, not sources.** The item never *granted* the power — it dramatizes what the person already is. If you are naturally focused on multiple personalities, it is
irrelevant whether you hold the Puzzle: you had years of experience being yourself. Losing the object is like being without a shirt — you continue being who you are.

## ⛓️ Binding why moving power unbalances

An item bound to a person for a while means that person is **experienced at using it** — they know where to pause, which height to fly. Moving it to someone else **unbalances the system**:

> **Give wings to a snake.** Venom and versatility balanced the lack of legs and arms. Adding flight creates a too-powerful animal — incredible, but it attracts a lot of attention.

**Ground Pokémon in the sky.** The thief steals the power but lacks the trained instincts. Snakes never learned how to behave in the air; birds were trained to fly since birth — aware of
where to pause, which height to fly. The same way you stole the item, someone else can now see *you* flying — an experienced ground Pokémon in a domain it never learned.

The game aims for **as many distinct powers and players as possible** — you cannot simply put everything in one individual without a cost. Balance is a constant pursuit to avoid large problems.

## 🃏 Unfair edges parallel monopolies

Two unfair starting positions framing a rivalry that **claims** to be pure strategy:

- Yugi holds the sole Millennium Puzzle.
- Kaiba holds the sole Blue-Eyes White Dragon + the best corporate print access.

**Fair within rules, unfair in life outcomes** — jet entrances and corporate stages are legal but are *not* card advantage. The rules can balance what happens *inside* the game; they cannot balance
what happens outside it.

**Print run vs strategy:** wealth buys the **currency**, not the **skill**. The grind route (limited pool, strong lines) can outplay the spoiled route (best cards, weak synergy) — Joey proves it. But
God cards and exclusive prints still gate the endgame: the print run returns at the top. Cross-ref [society.md § competition path](../society/society.md).

## 💰 Distinct-power economy

Spread distinct powers across players rather than stacking them in one individual.

- **No perfect score** — how are you studying *and* going to the gym at the same time? Someone who focused solely on the gym probably outplays the all-in-one.
- **Higher score → more attention** — the more you stack, the more eyes land on you.
- **Convergence → reset** — when power funnels upward until it is narratively unsustainable (all God cards + multiple Items on one person), the board must be **emptied** (Egypt: memory,
  self-confrontation, Items scatter or go dormant). The reset is the system's answer to over-accumulation.
- **Power concentration → prison** — concentrated power attracts a counter-force and eventually becomes a cage: everything gets spent sealing Zorc inside the Puzzle. *Cost of kingship.* Political
  collapse is what scatters the Items in the first place.
- **Perfect score → target** — a person who scored 10 on all tests attracts attention — becoming a **target of death**. Topping every ladder invites the one thing you can't defend.
- **Status inversion** — when someone at the **bottom** of a ranking becomes rich, someone at the **top** can lose their **purpose**: the position's whole meaning was the **gap**. Relative standing
  flips, not wealth.
- **The resourceless** — what about those **without money and without a lever**? At least a lever would make someone happy — which makes you happy. Nothing to trade and nothing to be wanted for is the
  deepest floor.

## 🔀 Rollback vs forward-only

Why not simply **rollback changes like a git commit** — launch something, then revert?

- The game would always be **stuck** until it finds the perfect version, which **does not exist**.
- So it goes **forward always, while allowing changes** — create a millennium item, swap hands to see what happens, then give back.
- **Time travel could revert a commit** — not fully sure why it is not done. Maybe the meta must keep moving.

Patch over rework, forward over perfection.

## 🎮 League meta loop

The constant pursuit of balance from the organizers, League-of-Legends style:

```text
too strong → banned in matches → useless → nerfed → too weak → unused → buffed
→ banned again
```

- **Never delete a character** — some people love it and would stop playing if you simply deleted it. Easier to **nerf** it if needed.
- **Power ceiling is narrative, not stable meta** — the strongest cards are as strong as the story needs; the meta shifts when the ceiling breaks.
- **One-patch tuning** — a single synergy card can fix a broken build *for one duel only*; then it must actually be balanced ("balance your deck"). Patches are band-aids, not design.
- **Tournament ideal** — the most balanced combination is best, allowing lots of characters without complaints about ban or weakness: *as if all were equally powerful.*

### 🗓️ Seasons — acknowledge, don't delete

The game engine outlives any one season. Strong cards **always exist**; collecting and deleting them ends the competition — the opposite of what a running championship needs.

- **Season cap** — the first season proposed the engine and hit its ceiling at the **three God cards**. New seasons start from the **established game** and add a little, but
  they are mostly **combinations of season one**: you cannot go much further than ATK and DEF, and the first season already had infinite ATK/DEF and tricks.
- **Same course, new class** — people are born and must study the game the same way, so someone has to keep it running; a new season is the old course with new students.
- **Control improves later** — the crazy shit shrinks from season 0 → 1 → GX; each season starts **easier to referee** than the last.
- **Acknowledge over delete** — banning the top cards makes the championship repetitive and meaningless; admitting they exist keeps it attractive.

## ⚔️ Stakes the heaven/hell war

**Endless war between heaven and hell** — regardless of which characteristics are defined as heaven or hell. A line with opposite sides. Team read:
[structures.md § hell vs heaven](./structures.md#%EF%B8%8F-hell-vs-heaven--two-teams-not-afterlife).

- **Pure heaven = WALL·E communism** — robot-managed equality: no crime, no competition, no sentiment. Then someone appears smarter, another prettier → **competition**.
  *Why should I live equally to someone prettier?* Unfairness creates hatred → crime → those at heaven call it hell. Cross-ref [futures.md § attractors](../society/futures.md).
- **Compensation rule** — if your girl chose the guy with the largest dick to be her favorite, you must be compensated in some manner. Otherwise, *what prevents you from killing that person?*
- **Dick economics** — large-dick guys tend poorer, small-dick tend richer. If the large-dick dude becomes rich, it brings up unfairness. The poor small-dick person has nothing
  preventing him from attacking the rich. The rich big-dick becomes vulnerable — you are probably busy enjoying life while the other is busy crafting a perfect plan.
  - **Worked example — Cariani / Balestrin:** Julio has the larger dick → women prefer him for sex → Cariani's counter must be **richer than him**. If Julio ever gets richer, nothing prevents Cariani
    from killing him — the compensation rule made literal.
When you win all competitions you become a target for losing something you **can't recover** (what if Cariani cuts Julio's dick off?). Society naturally drifts toward that escalation:
**Julio must always stay less wealthy than Cariani, or he risks losing his dick.**
  - **The protection equilibrium:** it's almost as if Cariani's job is to keep Julio **protected and as rich as possible** — Julio's wealth is the *excuse* for Cariani being very rich (just a bit
    more). If Julio goes poor, Cariani's floor drops too: his
minimum fundamental is **richer than Julio**, not rich in absolute terms. Dirty-name Julio (no credit) is fine as long as clean-name Cariani holds the credit — but **better to be both rich**. The
rival is a resource, not just a threat.
  - **Lifestyle asymmetry / the winner's cost:** little real difference between one million and a few; Julio probably enjoys life *more* — sex with Cariani's wife while Cariani rests on the larger
    boat. Winning locks you on the boat; the "loser" consumes the reward.
(Echoes [society § winner's sentiment load](./../society/society.md#-winners-vs-sentiment-bandwidth).) Sometimes people get out of control and someone **dies without being required** — or gets hurt
*by accident*, if they prefer saying.
- **Chaos needs one individual** — generally stimulated by unfairness.
- **Communism dead-end** — seems the solution, but only possible in a place where everyone is physically equal. Then you have exams: despite appearances, some are smarter than
  others. You do not need to be smart to pick up a gun and fire at the smartest; the smartest still needs to buy food and can be sabotaged by the stupider ones.
- **Balance → peace.** Balance somewhat guarantees happiness for everyone, which means peace. Imbalance brings unfairness → anger → war.
- **God with real power?** If god truly had powers, what is the **need for hell or police**? Can't criminals simply be stopped? Can't hunger be ended? People already have **tranquilizers** and
  **infinite wealth by reinvesting** — they simply **don't want to collaborate**. Read: power is **withheld, not absent** — the jurisdiction is the duel only ([referee §
  jurisdiction](#%EF%B8%8F-jurisdiction-is-the-duel-only)).

## 🛡️ Referee the secure environment

**Fair game is fiction.** The fair frame is an ideal that never fully holds — power always smuggles in via Items, cheating, or wealth. The championship world *runs on* the fair-game frame, not because
it is real, but because it is the **agreement**. Pricing the acts: [fair-game.md](./fair-game.md).

### 🏆 Championship as post-war treaty

The rules are the **consequence of a previous war agreement** — regardless of who had won the war, the championship tends to continue. A new war begins when people realize they were being fooled.

**Why not any armed person?** If it were about unfairness, anyone with a weapon would win. The championship only means something *because* the frame bans that: it decides who is strongest
**under the rules**, not strongest period.

**Bounded game** — standard cards, published rules, tournament brackets. What the frame requires to be a championship:

- **Guaranteed security** — provided by others outside who monitor people: acknowledging who should own each item and eventually giving it back; acknowledging who should hold each card.
- **ID on each card** — attach each card to a specific user. The ownership registry is what gives the championship meaning: *what is it about if anyone can print a Blue-Eyes?*

### 🛡️ Kaiba as protector

Kaiba is the individual who protects others from sabotage — ensuring people can compete without being interfered with. As long as what you have is a deck of valid cards, anything is possible.

**Off-bracket sabotage** — cheaters attack the *frame itself*, not just the match: Siegfried hacks the DuelTek computer; Weevil steals Exodia; Marik controls people with tools. The referee must
protect the tournament system, and the monitor itself can be compromised.

**Marik's manipulation** — Rod control, kidnapping a family and threatening them to obey during a duel. The duel *seems* fair at first glance but is quite manipulated. It attracted Yugi's attention
and eventually got grounded by him.

**Custodian/watchdog layer** — Shadi tests *worthiness* (judgment via Scales, forced self-view via Key), guards the meaning of the Items, and never transfers them. A watchdog layer that never fully
closes.

### ⚖️ Jurisdiction is the duel only

The frame covers the **game**, not life:

- What if Yugi loses a limb **out of a duel**? Kaiba does not know how to fix health problems.
- Who deserves to suffer, and why? Unanswerable within the frame.
- What prevents people from simply using a **kitchen knife** against each other? Nothing in the rules — the secure environment encloses the match, not life.

### 🔧 The fix layer — eventual fixes

Very hard to **prevent** cheaters from cheating, but somewhat easy to **pinpoint** them and prevent them from participating. Being barred means trouble joining again — which somewhat fixes the
problem.

- **Exclusion over prevention** — ban the cheater; re-entry is costly.
- **Traceability, not prevention** — hard to stop a card being stolen, easy to *ping where it was last used*. Value by rarity of loss: lose a Kuriboh → nothing to complain; lose the Obelisk → quite
  troublesome.
- **Value is frame-conferred** — a card only has value *inside* the official tournament. Unofficial tournaments allow anything — why not pick up a paper and print the card? Even a perfect copy is
  worthless: *fake money for fake food*. This is why the registry works: the frame is the source of value.
- **Copy symmetry** — if someone can generate a perfect copy, the owner can also generate a new one. What matters is **authorization** — who is *allowed* to use each card — not who physically holds
  it.
- **The audience is the flag** — everyone who follows the championship knows the owner. If Obelisk appears played by anyone but Kaiba, it raises eyebrows. Exclusivity is the anti-cheat: Kaiba crafted
  a strategy where no one else could use the Blue-Eyes, forcing people to fight for good cards.
- **Envy as theft motive** — a stolen card was stolen because someone wanted its value, or wanted *you* not to have it.
- **Theft only pays in the valueless space** — stealing a card only means you can play against your neighbors in an unfair duel. No one cares about such garbage. People generally like to watch the
  fair duel, and only one person can play that card during such duels.
- **Embodied identity as the real anti-theft** — you can steal something and fake a perfect lie, but *who believes you*? If Kaiba was the only one who could play Obelisk, how could you fake
  him? Quite a few surgeries would be required — in practice not possible yet, or very very rare. Credibility is frame-bound; the registry is backed by **bodies**, not paper.

**Recovery = reissue** — they can simply craft a new original card and give it back to the owner. The major problem is when the *owner* is kidnapped — the person, not the object.

**Remaining vulnerability = fear** — kidnap and physical damage remain a problem: what if your family is under some threat during the duel? Abstract to simply
*being afraid of losing something important to you* — this makes the duel unfair, and the frame cannot fix fear.

## 🗣️ Politics at cost of privacy

One option when attention rises is to be **politic** — at the cost of privacy. A barrel with some holes and just a few band-aids: there is always some hole open that someone else may have worked
harder to protect better. Related: [monitoring.md](../perception/monitoring.md), [masks.md](../perception/masks.md).

**Localized fame** — high score attracts attention, but the champion can keep fame in the friend circle (Yugi) rather than mass celebrity — the politic option in practice.

## ❓ Open questions

- **Honest duel with an item active** — can a duel count as honest if a Millennium Item is active? Championship ideal says no Items; practice says Items define the highest-stakes duels. Honesty is
  aspirational rhetoric.
- **Item succession** — who should wield the Items after the original holder leaves? Maybe the wrong frame: Items belong to an era, and the modern world keeps cards, not priesthood. Still unclear why
  one Item stays as a personal symbol when all others deactivate.
