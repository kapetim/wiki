# 🎲 Free will — the unpredictable player

**What this is:** an **abstract** frame for agency — why a next action can be *forecast* only while character holds,
why a change of state breaks any model built on the previous state, and why free will and corruption are two faces of
the same coin. **Metaphor only.** Referee layer: [balance.md](./balance.md) · rules + judge: [structures.md](./structures.md) ·
watching: [monitoring.md](../perception/monitoring.md).

---

## 🎯 Predictable only until it changes

A prediction is a bet on the *next action* from the *last action*. That bet pays only while the player's
**character is fixed** — a narrow distribution, a near-certain move. The model is not reading truth; it is reading
**the absence of change**.

Same input, different output, the moment the state changes. Forecasting is therefore **cheap for rule-followers**
and **cheap to defeat** for anyone willing to wear a different skin.

## 🎭 The mask is a state change

A role is a **skin on the same body** ([masks.md](../perception/masks.md)). Cook, courier, caretaker, or threat can
all be one week of one player. Each skin is a **state**, not a new person, so a model keyed to the last skin misreads
the next. What looks like acting out of character is usually **another character being worn**.

## 🔁 Free will implies corruption

Close the door on deviation and you get a **world with no crime and no free will** — an obedient machine, and a
referee with nothing to referee. Corruption is the **price of agency**: the freedom that lets a player cook also lets
them deviate, and the deviation is where fairness is tested ([corruption.md](../../problems/unknown/corruption.md),
[fair-game.md](./fair-game.md)).

## ⚖️ The judgment domino

A deviation does not stay one move. The actor moves; the enforcer can wave it off; the judge can ignore it; and each
wave makes the next cheaper — a cascade, not an event ([structures.md](./structures.md),
[judgement.md](../perception/judgement.md)).

## 🧩 Wrong player, right story

Because the crowd wants a **face** and a **suffering**, the resolved story and the actual act drift apart: a liked
actor is waved through, an absent player can be cast as the culprit. The verdict tracks the **story**, not the
**state change**.
