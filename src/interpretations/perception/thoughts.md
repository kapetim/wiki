# 🧠 Thoughts — the always-streaming sim, unchecked

**What this is:** a raw stream-of-thought about living as a monitored sim — live-streaming as a form of security, thoughts as an uncontrollable process, evidence in the AI age, and the corner cases
where proof collapses. **Metaphor only.** Pairs with [monitoring.md](monitoring.md).

---

## 📺 Live stream as security

Live streaming is like a vaccine against disease: it helps more than raw direct contact, but some diseases are stronger than it. HIV has management but no cure; COVID simply appeared and caught lots
of people without one. Still, most other diseases barely affect the body.

A live stream somewhat represents the Truman-show life — not immune to violence and irritation, but generally more protected than someone without it. A person takes some time to reach the conclusion
to attack you during a live stream; others without it are more appealing targets. It's some kind of security in trade for privacy — just odd to get used to, not that life changing.

If you are living like Truman, you've probably been monitored since birth. It's not like you had privacy to begin with. At best you forget about it and live in some alternate reality with privacy —
like in the bathroom.

## 👁️ Eyesight, thoughts, and the process

It may be that life is a game and your eyesight is shared across other players, as if you were holding a camera. Getting blind is simply stupid — losing the most important resource due to a silly
concern.

Thoughts are probably the same shit. If you're a bot, it's like a computer process generating text messages from time to time. If anyone can watch your eyesight, they can also read your thoughts —
even if you use imagination to represent them as
images or videos. Accepting it as some reality is the first step; fixing it is probably impossible, more likely to get nuts fighting an infinite battle without a chance to win. In The Sims, your sim
can't hide anything from you.

A sim that realizes this is not granted anything special — it's more like a consequence depending on what you do. Consuming cannabis makes a person wonder about possibilities; they created The Matrix
but never explained it. Someone high may wonder
too much, go out, and collect validations — noises synchronized with their thoughts, words close to some unique shit they wondered. Very hard to prove; at best you have data points that make you look
paranoid rather than smart.

Even if you're right, there's nothing a sim can do about it — maybe the game owner just pays more attention to these players, like a ninja within the Naruto world. Those who couldn't adapt to knowing
may just be standard civilians.

Wondering too much is not very helpful. It somewhat provides a sense of fairness — without it, people get away with any well-crafted crime. Assume crimes happen, but someone keeps listening
as an excuse to offer revenge paths. An assassination in an unmonitored place leaves no trace; if the criminal is good at self-management, others never realize their past.

With monitoring, a set of people would know — like putting a warning if any individual dies, collecting the timestamp and evaluating the scene, pretty much like soccer VAR. If it's a video game, you
can collect the event data and rewatch it from many angles.

## 💾 Evidence and the data-on-disk problem

It's very hard to delete a single entry from a database without a log. You can throw out an HDD, but the game manager saw you writing to it and throwing it out — not like they lost it. You would
depend on someone sabotaging the game log, which would mean the game is very corrupt.

There are not that many crimes without a single trace; it's almost stamped in the criminal's face. You probably have a list of suspects and can easily throw pebbles to see their reaction — very hard
to lie many times in a row without mistakes.

The Zodiac is a corner case: lots of clues pointing back, but not a single valid proof — people would need assumptions to jail him. If you put pictures of the
victims and evaluated habits when facing evidence, they could show signals of involvement. A cold individual can match most clues and say nothing.

Today the same can happen: a crime during a night club with the energy cut off leaves many candidates and little evidence. Only with video-game VAR is it possible — but then you can re-generate event
footage in many variations, like a shirt re-colored in many ways. You can't say which is true.

A picture is a matrix of numbers; changing colors is replacing numbers. As long as it looks realistic, it's valid — and it's now hard to distinguish true video from AI-generated. A
person with enough money can craft evidence without a connection: record a realistic video and edit a few frames — change the shooter's face to accuse someone else.

How can you prove it's valid? The audio is original, only a tiny change in the video. Record the same video twice and you have two distinct valid proofs; a third can be edited. Who can say a video is
not real?

What if the criminal wears a mask and gloves — realistic evidence without their face shown? What if the energy was cut off — no evidence at all? What if the mask color changes slightly? As long as the
data is programmatically
valid, it's impossible to say. Video and audio footage is not good evidence — you can record it once and store it, copy it, or generate it. Alternative video is finding a combination of numbers that
other people confirm.

What is left is raw interview footage — follow someone and check habits. A psychopath like Zodiac shows no evidence: five minutes of odd behavior during a crime, never again. It's
possible to stress them for more data, but with a gun pointed at you, it's about saying what the person wants to hear — better jail than killed, even if innocent.

People generally aren't that good at lying; each crime leaves clues in the person's habit, like what they did right after the event. If a crime happened at the bottom of a labyrinth and you were close
to the entrance, it's very hard to go against the water flow.

## 🧩 DID and personality

Worth adding some personal DID interpretation. It seems similar to unlocking the Sharingan in the Uchiha — when a person loses their personality and becomes a sponge of patterns, like
Ditto in Pokémon. You don't know who you are exactly, who to believe, what to do. In general people try to persuade others to do what they want; but what do you want to do?

DID introduces an all-in-one package — a collection of personalities. You can kill someone and go to church right after. It's like three very distinct personality
traits; easier to manage from a third-person view — behave like an assassin, then a bishop, then a student. Who controls which personality is current?

Ditto is reactive — becomes the exact copy of the other. There may be differences, like becoming the opposite to exploit a weakness — a XOR of the other's personality. If
it's neither chosen nor reactive to the situation, it's automatic mode programmed by someone else — like a computer running processes that have been started.

## 🎛️ Obedience and control

Who controls what you do? If you're a bot in automated mode, why aren't you disciplined? Why do some pass hours studying while others can't? It seems tied to obedience — doing exactly what the person
you respect the most says, with respect tied to a
fear of rejection. Joy comes from accomplishing forbidden tasks. A crime is like playing against police; being caught is going to jail. What about a crime in a place without internet or electricity —
is everyone a robot with Starlink?

Most people obey some church shit — parents or grandparents went to church, so you obey through them or god directly. If you don't believe in a god form, you may be
agnostic — independent-seeming, but still dependent on capitalism. Perhaps the most independent goes to communism, then depends on the government.

The most independent person has the fewest layers of obedience — like a CEO — but also the highest risk of being responsible for all others. Sometimes being in the middle is great, or having no
one below you and a doable routine. Children create a 20-year contract of responsibility — five years of low money but high time/energy, then gradually more money and less time.

Not very fulfilling — like training a dog for a competition. Only relevant if you like being a teacher or are a needy person; you could have played a video game instead, maintained a Neopet for 20
years, or raised a kid.

## 🔬 Substance and the flow

Note about substance usage: sometimes the belly is bad after food, sometimes not; a few days you're happy, some others sad; the same movie may not be cool every time; coffee sometimes feels strong
despite the same
quantity. It's like a river flow — despite looking similar, the water always changes. Full control over the brain would let you get things done — like a computer running the same script twice.
