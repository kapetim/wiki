## 📺 Flintstones vs Jetsons (issue read)

They live **very similar domestic lives** — marriage, kids, job, sitcom reset. Most difference is **resource abundance**:

<!-- begin table -->
| Pole | Tech | Risk | Blind spot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Flintstones** | Stones, animals powering appliances | Dinosaur more likely than car | Cannot see evolution ahead |
| **Jetsons** | Spaceships, Rosie, automation | Robot misalignment (cleaner grabs gun) | Obesity, boredom — humans won survival, lost craft |
<!-- end table -->

Jetsons looks beautiful until WALL·E: people obese and stupid, machines doing all work — not murder, but **passengers**.

Reality sits **between**: some pursue evolution, some pursue going back. Each new tool trashes the last:

```text
Abacus → Calculator → Spreadsheet → AI markdown tables → …
```

## 💣 Automation's force edge

Beyond comfort, automation carries a **military / force edge**:

- **No mundane work needed** — robots cook, clean, run everything; the danger is **I-Robot / Matrix**: tools get too good and end up battling humans.
- **A perfect tool can be used against you** — machines are **more resistant and easier to replicate** than fragile bone-humans; an **army of robots beats an army of humans**.
- **The "Jedi window"** — a phase where robots are still stupid and humans look strong; once robots get smart enough, humans lose.
- **Asymmetric war examples** — code a swarm of drones to carry a bomb to another country; without anti-bomb defense, you lose. A hardcoded tank that simply moves forward and fires causes huge trouble
  **unless someone nukes it**. Enough such tanks can win a war **without a human being there**.
