# 🧮 Fair game — the judgement system

**What this is:** the **judgement system** — it **collects evidence** and **applies previously defined costs**. Pairs
with [karma.md](karma.md), the **accounting system**: karma keeps the ledger, fair game runs the trial that prices the
act. An **abstract** frame for fairness — treat each player as a **manual car**: fully controllable and
able to take any action, including the worst. Fair game is the *organization* of what counts as fair through a
**rules system**, and its job is to put a **cost / charge** on the acts that freedom allows. **Metaphor only.**
Referee layer: [balance.md](../framework/balance.md) · rules + judge: [structures.md](../framework/structures.md) · freedom:
[free-will.md](../framework/free-will.md).

---

## 🚗 The manual-car premise

Assume a player can do **anything** — no built-in governor, no automatic lane-keeping. Agency is the wheel, the
clutch, and the throttle. A "good" world is not one where nobody can cross the line; it is one where crossing the
line has a **price** ([free-will.md](../framework/free-will.md)).

## 📒 Rules define fair

Rules — law, league, treaty, taboo — are the thing that **defines** what counts as fair, and they do nothing on
their own. They need a **judge** to confirm a result, so a score stands or a rematch is served
([structures.md](../framework/structures.md)). Without that confirmation the book stays open and the round never closes.

## 🧾 Price, not prohibition

Fair game does not remove the act; it **charges** for it. A price is a ledger: the act is allowed, the cost is
stated, and the consequence is **predictable** rather than personal. This is the difference between a rule and a
grudge.

## 🪞 Fairness is the ledger that holds

A system is **sustainable when its ledger holds** — when the price applies evenly and the referee is not the corrupt
party ([corruption.md](../../problems/unknown/corruption.md)). Fairness is not a feeling; it is whether the **same
act draws the same charge**, whoever commits it.

## 🧭 Establishment

To establish a fair game: **state the rules**, **staff an independent referee**, **confirm results**, and **publish
the price**. Each step is also its own failure mode — an unstated rule, a hired referee, an unconfirmed result, a
secret price ([balance.md](../framework/balance.md), [monitoring.md](../monitoring.md)).

## 🔗 Fairness ≈ karma

Fairness and karma are close cousins — both demand an **equal response** to an act. Both are **stimulated by
competition with lack of self-control**: competition sets the expectation of getting what you deserve; lack of
self-control is why **people naturally commit crimes** in the first place.

<!-- begin table -->
| | Read |
| --- | --- |
| **Fairness** | Equal response to acts — the ledger **balances** |
| **Karma** | The cosmic name for the same hope: it balances **eventually** |
| **Fuel** | Competition (you compare your slice) + low self-control (crimes happen anyway) |
<!-- end table -->

## ⚔️ Vengeance — the default justice provider

No god keeps the ledger, so **people provide the eventual justice themselves** — vengeance is the closest thing karma
has to an enforcer on Earth. It explains how "justice" arrives without a referee
([society.md § fix, justice, vengeance](../society/society.md)).

**While there's vengeance, there's someone sad about unfairness.**

## 🐕 Collared vengeance

Vengeance stimulates a lot of people — it's a **strong engine**. But the people running it must be **angry dogs with a
collar**: channeled, bounded, pointed only where the rules allow. Otherwise people **transform complaints into war** —
and **everyone loses**. The norm is the same: seek fix and justice before vengeance.

## 💥 No fair game — first to kill wins

When the game stops being fair, **anything is allowed** — the first to kill the other has already won. It can happen
anytime, without a warning: a gunshot, your home exploded while you sleep. There is **no guarantee fairness will
arrive**.

- **Serial killers** craft **unrecoverable unfairness** — no ledger can balance their score.
- **Karma, at best, is anger from the still-alive** pursuing revenge — making the killer suffer bad sensations for a
  period, until it *feels* fair. It is never equal: **2 kills is already worse than dying yourself**.
- Anything else is a **fake fairness system** — revenge for something you didn't do.

## ⚖️ Punishment vs the act

Two gunshots is an **instant kill** — seconds of pain. Forty years in jail is a very different price. Still, it's the
rule invented. The jail system is **biased toward violent offenses**; assassination is treated oddly — spare time
with full security in a cell.

## 🎯 Perfect balance — hard to achieve

Perfect balance is great, but **very hard to reach**.

- A man wins the lottery — a large paycheck and lots of money. Not a criminal, but a **target** for the poor.
- A terminal-cancer individual has **nothing to lose** — pursuing an assassination right before the graveyard is a
  rational move for them.

## ⚖️ Law's rough ranking (person ↔ person)

Law is strong at **ranking** which human-on-human harms society treats as worst. It mirrors the severity scale in
[judgement.md](../judgement.md) — infraction → misdemeanor → felony → capital. The order is **stable across many
countries** even when labels differ.

<!-- begin table -->
| Tier (metaphor) | Examples | Why law cares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Capital / irreversible** | Murder, assassination | Victim's run ended |
| **Felony-class** | Grievous assault, serious bodily offenses, arson with risk, large fraud | Lasting body, liberty, or livelihood damage |
| **Misdemeanor-class** | Battery, DUI, repeat theft, some harassment | Real harm, recoverable life often |
| **Infraction / petty** | Minor theft, disorderly conduct, many traffic sins | Nuisance + deterrence |
<!-- end table -->

**Animals, plants, insects** — law **can** punish cruelty, poaching, environmental damage, but the **default moral
weight in courts and headlines** is usually **below** comparable **human** victim harm. A slaughterhouse is legal;
kicking a dog may be misdemeanor; killing a person is felony/capital. **Do not confuse "hard to prosecute" with
"harmless."** The **state's priority ladder** and your **personal ledger** may diverge from how you feel about
non-human harm.

## 🧾 Proof — what courts can use

Courts need **admissible evidence**, not vibes. Without it, the case often **never opens** or **loses**.

<!-- begin table -->
| Harm type | Often provable when… | Often not provable when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Public violence** | witnesses, cameras, injuries, 911 timeline | masked, no cameras, victim won't testify |
| **Theft / fraud** | bank records, contracts, digital trail | cash, verbal deal, no paper |
| **DUI / traffic** | breath test, cam, officer report | — (usually public stop) |
| **Betrayal / cheating** | rare — texts, photos, admissions | private words only; he said / she said |
| **Exposure risk** | medical records + traceable contact if tested | consensual private contact, no clinic trail |
| **Gossip / reputation** | defamation needs a false fact + harm + malice | insult, opinion, whisper network |
<!-- end table -->

**Practical immunity of private acts:** if it happened **behind closed doors**, with **no messages, witnesses, or
hospital paper**, the **legal** risk to the actor is often **low** — not zero (confessions, later leaks, pattern
cases), but **low**. That is not moral permission; it is **epistemic** reality.

**Public or documented acts** invert the odds: street fight, posted video, workplace email, bank transfer —
**proof finds you**.

## 🏠 Street ledger — belief without trial

People on the street **do not wait for conviction**.

<!-- begin table -->
| Mechanism | Needs proof? | Cost to you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gossip** | No — repetition = "true enough" | Jobs, friends, dating pool |
| **Cold shoulder** | No | Isolation |
| **Threat / violence** | No — wrong crowd may act on rumor | Body risk |
| **Family / partner exit** | Often behavioral pattern, not court | Housing, support |
| **Employer soft fire** | HR rarely needs beyond suspicion | Income |
<!-- end table -->

So: **"got away with it in court"** (or never charged) **≠** **"got away with it in life"**. Exposure risks and
betrayal especially hit the **body + trust** ledgers even when law is blind.

## 💵 Total cost (prize vs price)

```text
Total cost ≈ legal exposure + health/body + money + time
 + trust destroyed + street reputation + personal karma
```

<!-- begin table -->
| Act (examples) | Law (if private) | Body | Street / trust |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unprotected contact, undisclosed risk | Usually low proof | **Health and pregnancy** — real | Partner/friend judgment if story spreads |
| Secret affair | Rarely criminal | Emotional | High if exposed socially |
| Petty lie to friend | None | — | Medium when pattern seen |
| Public assault | **High** | High | High |
<!-- end table -->

Optimize for **total cost**, not "will a judge see this?"

## 🤝 Who to believe — trustability

When stories conflict (accusation, gossip, your own excuse):

<!-- begin table -->
| Signal | Trust more | Trust less |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Costly behavior over time** | They act consistent when lying would be easier | Words without follow-through |
| **Corroboration** | Independent records, third parties who lose nothing | Single anonymous source |
| **Incentive** | Speaker risks something by saying it | Speaker gains money, status, revenge |
| **Specificity** | Dates, places, falsifiable details | Vague moral theater |
| **Pattern** | Same story from multiple eras | One-off when drama peaks |
| **Your hunger** | You slow down when you *want* it true | You accept because it flatters or frightens you |
<!-- end table -->

**Default stance:**

1. **Believe enough to adjust margins** — sleep elsewhere, stop lending money, get tested, rotate passwords —
   without needing a jury.
2. **Do not treat unproven private harm as public fact** — avoid becoming the next gossip node.
3. **Trust behavior > speech.**

**Exposure / betrayal specifically:** treat **medical and logistical facts** (test results, disclosed partners,
timelines) as primary; treat **character verdicts** as slow-burn pattern reads, not one argument.

## 🗂️ Case log — the read

Examples to remember: **it takes quite some effort to prove someone is wrong, and they naturally get away with it.**

**Read:** monitored data and illegally collected evidence are **two different kinds of proof** — total visibility does
not mean usable in court ([evidence.md](evidence.md)). A cruelty case may never reach jail; at best it draws private
vengeance, limited to a single individual who cared.

## 📦 Possession vs distribution — risk tiers

The biggest legal split isn't what you have — it's **who else is involved**. An act kept to yourself is rarely
prosecuted; **distribution is the charge**. The victimless principle: a private act that involves nobody else is hard
to bring to court — the moment it is sold or shared, it is a different game.

## 🤝 The fairness contract

The hierarchy only holds when players face each other with **equal opportunity**. Anyone could strike a sleeping
opponent — but that **opts out of the game**, and a fair match shows potential rather than circumstance. An
**integrity enforcer** watches for those who reject the contract and can answer in kind.

## 🎯 Vengeance queue — priority dispatch

Justice runs as a queue: a harm arrives, gets investigated, is routed to an executor, and the result is reviewed. A
cost can be **repriced by consensus** — the wheel keeps spinning either way.

<!-- begin table -->
| Step | Read |
| --- | --- |
| Receive news | A harm is reported to the collector |
| Investigate | Establish who is responsible |
| Route | Assign an executor to pursue the target |
| Review | Check the result and dispatch feedback |
| Reprice | Consensus can change a cost — the wheel keeps spinning |
<!-- end table -->
